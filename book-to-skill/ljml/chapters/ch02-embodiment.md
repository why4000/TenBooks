# Chapter 2: 媒介的具身化

## Core Idea
Media are not objects added to a pre-existing subject; they are extensions of the phenomenal body. The body is the "元媒介" (meta-medium) — the most originary medium through which all other media are constituted.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **身体即元媒介 (Body as Meta-Medium)**: "身体是在世界上存在的媒介物" — to have a body is to be embedded in an environment, to have a perceptual and practical field.
  - When to use: when tracing media to their existential origin.
  - How: ask "what bodily limitation does this medium overcome?"
- **补余的逻辑 (Logic of Complementarity, 斯蒂格勒)**: the organism's programs extend into artificial programs; the extension is formed in the very process of extending — there is no interiority before extension.
- **具身现象学 (Embodied Phenomenology, 梅洛-庞蒂)**: the body is neither pure extended substance (广延实体) nor pure thinking substance (思维实体), but the original synthesis of both — the "phenomenal body".

## Key Concepts
- **现象身体**: body as lived from first-person perspective, not observable from third-person.
- **身体意向性**: the body's active directedness toward the world.
- **技术意向性 (唐·伊德)**: technologies inherit and transform bodily intentionality; technology can occupy the "of" position in consciousness-of-something.

## Mental Models
- "水之于鱼，正如媒介之于我们" — media are an environment, like water to fish; we don't notice them because they permeate our "attention field".
- The body-world relation is like a Möbius strip — no inside/outside distinction.

## Anti-patterns
- **新瓶装旧酒**: labeling something "embodied" without grasping its phenomenological meaning.
- **把身体当对象**: treating the body as a measurable object rather than lived experience.

## Key Takeaways
1. Media originate from the body's need to overcome its limitations.
2. 对象媒介 separates from the body only in theory; in lived experience, media always remain tied to bodily perception.
3. 具身化 is not just "the body is involved" — it is the claim that cognition and communication are fundamentally bodily.

- **Ch 3**: the five features of 现象媒介 are derived from embodiment.
